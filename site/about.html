How it works

cafe answers one question before a field deployment: will this fleet of underwater, surface and aerial vehicles do its job together, in the weather that day actually had?

  1. Describe each vehicle

    A few dimensions or a CAD file, plus mass, power and battery.

  2. Characterize it once

    OpenFOAM for drag and hull lift, Capytaine for added mass, CalculiX for collapse depth and loads.

  3. Fly the plan

    Every vehicle in real WRF wind, HYCOM currents and WAVEWATCH III seas, many times with the uncertain inputs varied.

  4. Read the verdict

    Go, marginal or no-go for every vehicle and every range requirement, with the reason.

The chart and the full simulator

The chart page runs a quick check in your browser: each vehicle moves at the speed its CFD-derived power curve allows, is carried by the real current or wind, and spends its battery accordingly, under the same failure rules and the same GO / MARGINAL / NO-GO thresholds as the full simulator. The three vehicles are example designs, not a real fleet.

What the quick check simplifies

  • Fixed two-hour mission. Every run is two hours long, whatever you change on the chart.
  • Fewer, different runs. The browser counts 23 varied runs per verdict (a 24th, plain run is drawn but not counted); the full simulator counted 15. The thresholds are the same (GO needs 90 % of runs and a 90 % Wilson lower bound of 0.75), but with different run counts and different input spreads (for example the current error is 0.05 m/s here and 0.10 m/s there) the same plan can land on the other side of a line.
  • No waves on the hull. The browser knows the wave height (it fails the boat above its design limit) but not what waves do to its power, so it reports far more battery left on the boat than the full simulator does.
  • Weather edges. Weather outside the wind grid (7.07 to 17.76° N, 100.98 to 88.27° W) is not extrapolated: the nearest edge value is used. The current and wave grids are a little different in extent and are held at their edges the same way.
  • Coarse seafloor. Land and depth come from a 4-minute (about 7 km) grid, so shores and shoals are approximate.

Proof

Every claim is checked against a formula or a published measurement. Rows marked for context never count.
