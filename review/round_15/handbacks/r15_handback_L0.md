## L0-2. Two jobs that take over one stale CPU claim at once get the same cores, and one can die

DECISIONS row: The claim phase of `bin/proot_run.sh` (choose cores, claim each with `mkdir ~/cafe-worker/cafe/tmp/cpu.<n>`, take over a stale claim) runs under `flock` on `~/cafe-worker/cafe/tmp/cpulock`, released before the job starts so that the job does not inherit the lock; the file is never removed (a second file of that name would be a second lock), and a worker without `flock` says so and claims unlocked as before. The takeover of a stale claim is "read its pid, `rm -rf`, `mkdir`": three steps on a path, not on the claim that was judged, and the K jobs of a gate start together and share one least-busy order, so they reach the same stale claim. Two jobs that had read the same dead pid each removed the other's new claim and pinned to the same two cores, and the later `echo $$ > cpu.<n>/pid` under `set -e` found its directory gone and the job died (sandbox of the finding: 8 jobs over 16 stale claims, cores pinned twice in 4 rounds of 6, a job dead in 3). The "eight shares get distinct cores" of DECISIONS 840 held only when no claim was stale, which is the case after any SIGKILL or container restart.

Amends: row 840 (add "claims are made under flock, round 15 L0").

CHANGELOG: `bin/proot_run.sh` made the claims of CPU cores under `flock`: jobs started together over stale claims (left by a SIGKILLed wrapper or a restart) were pinned to the same cores, and one could die on a claim directory that another job had removed (round 15, look-ahead L0).

## L0-4. The line reader of the hull mesh number read a different number than PyYAML for the same file, and refused a valid key line (two findings, one defect)

Fix at the one place both come from, `cafe.fea.hull_elements_along()` (one caller, `hangar.fea_cases` line 172; `pipeline_windows._fea` reads the number through the pm loader, unchanged): the line reader now reads one layout and refuses every other, and a refusal is a `ValueError` that names the entry and the line.

DECISIONS row: `cafe.fea.hull_elements_along()` (the L0-1 reader of `stage3.fea_elements_along`, read without PyYAML because the app image has none) never returns a number that PyYAML would not return for the same file: it reads the key on a line of its own (a comment may follow), the lines at the indent of the entry's first child, exactly one `value:` among them, a decimal whole number with no sign, no leading zero and no `_` (so `070`, `70#x`, `+70`, `7_0` are refused), at most ` # comment` after it, and the key once in the file; lines deeper than the first child are the text of another child (a block scalar, a nested mapping) and are not read; a child that starts a flow collection, an anchor, a tag, an alias or a quoted text that does not end on its line is refused, since the lines under it could mean anything. Found by an adversary on ed75bf4: the first form split on `#` without a space (`70#x`, a text for PyYAML), took octal as decimal (PyYAML 56), took the first of two `value:` lines (PyYAML keeps the last), ignored a second entry of the key (PyYAML keeps the last), took a `value:` line inside a nested mapping or a block scalar placed before the real one (PyYAML 70, reader 5 or 9), and needed the key line to be exactly `stage3.fea_elements_along:` so that a comment or a trailing space on it (valid YAML, 70) ended every Hangar hull case in `ValueError`. All of these but the last returned a wrong hull mesh with no error; the Hangar would then have reported a wrong collapse depth. A flow mapping on the key line (`{value: 70, ...}`) is valid YAML and is refused here, with the reason in the message; the file in the repo is in the block layout and `test_r15_l0_the_hull_mesh_number_is_read_without_pyyaml_because_the_app_image_has_none` holds the reading to PyYAML's on it, so an edit to a layout the reader does not take fails in the pm image, not in the app image. The number is 70 before and after; no printed number of the demo moves.

Amends: the L0-1 row above (add "and held to PyYAML's number by a refusal of every layout it does not read, L0-4").

CHANGELOG: The Hangar's hull mesh number is read by a stricter reader: a line reader that took `070` for 70 (PyYAML 56), `70#x`, the first of two `value:` lines, or a `value:` line inside another child's text could mesh the hull at the wrong size and report a wrong collapse depth with no error, and a comment after the key refused the whole hull case; `cafe.fea.hull_elements_along` now returns only what PyYAML returns for the file, or says why it refuses the layout (round 15, look-ahead L0-4).
