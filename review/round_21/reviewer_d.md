### D-1. `PLAN_SIZE_MAX` = 10**6 is not a bound: a `plan.json` with `batch: 1000000` passes `_plan_sizes` and the what-if's memory grows without limit
severity: minor
lane: robustness
where: cafe/pm/__main__.py:309 (`PLAN_SIZE_MAX`), :320-325 (`_plan_sizes`); the real bounds are `budget.BATCH` (ci 16, quick 32, 4h 64, overnight 128, `budget.py:34`) and `budget.N_MAX` (16, 64, 256, 512, `budget.py` `N_MAX`)
reproduction: `/data/runs/rev21_d_s/t7.py` on a copy of q22 (`batch` is 32 in its `plan.json`): `python -m cafe.pm whatif --mission <run>/mission.yaml --run <run> --shift 1h`, once with the plan as made, once with `"batch": 1000000` edited in. RSS of the process and its children every 10 s: batch 32: 1.8, 4.5, 2.2, 2.3, 2.5, 2.2, 2.4, 2.5, 2.2, 2.4 GB (flat through 100 s). batch 1,000,000: 1.9, 5.5, 2.3, 3.5, 3.6, 5.7, 10.9, 16.1, 21.3, 26.5 GB at 10 ... 100 s, about 0.52 GB/s from 60 s on, still rising when I killed it by pid at 100 s (an earlier 91 s run of the same plan had peaked at 22 GB). `batch` of 1000001, 1e30, 10**30, 10**400 and `n_max` of 1000001 are refused as the prompt says (the 'damaged or incomplete' message names the bound; my harness recorded elapsed seconds, not the exit code). The check already holds the run's budget name (`name`, validated one line above) and `BATCH[name]` / `N_MAX[name]` are 128 and 512 at most: the bound is 2,000 to 8,000 times the largest value any plan has. (A valid `batch` can also exceed `n_max`; not checked either.)
why it matters: this is round 20's M-5 fix (DECISIONS 1022) and its stated purpose was that a damaged or hand-edited plan must not make a what-if ask for as many members as it takes; 10**6 still does, on the shared box, and only a user who knows to watch RSS stops it. Using `budget.BATCH[name]` and `budget.N_MAX[name]` as the bounds (or the larger of the old and the current table if older runs may differ) would close it with no new constant.
wrong data: no (resource hazard; nothing is printed that is wrong)

### D-2. `report --run` is not fixed for the damage round 20 fixed in `whatif`: an oversized integer in `plan.json` is still an OverflowError traceback (exit 1), where RUNBOOK 280 promises the 'damaged or incomplete' message (exit 2)
severity: minor
lane: robustness
where: cafe/pm/config.py:327 (`damaged_run_files` catches LookupError, ValueError, TypeError, AttributeError and not ArithmeticError); cafe/pm/budget.py:538 (`plan["batch"] * ...`), :530 (`plan["budget_s"] * ...`), :602 (`p['stage1_s']:.0f`); cafe/pm/__main__.py:221-229 (`_report`); docs/RUNBOOK.md:280
reproduction: `/data/runs/rev21_d_s/t6.py`, the same copy of q22, `python -m cafe.pm report --run <run>` with `plan.json` edited to `"batch": 1` + 400 zeros: `Traceback ... budget.py line 538, in _batch ... The same with `budget_s` (budget.py:530 in `emulator_share_s`) and `stage1_s` (budget.py:602 in `describe`, via `report._machine` line 1283) of 10**400. `whatif` with `batch` 10**400 gives the message (t3; exit code not recorded). A number up to 1.8e308 is fine; the failure starts at 10**309.

The root cause is the wrapper that every command relies on ("a file ... that holds another type where one is read is the same damage"): it does not treat an ArithmeticError as damage, so each numeric field of `plan.json` (and of every other run file) that a command multiplies is a sibling. ### D-4. A `gci` that is an integer above 1.8e308 passes the seed's new check, is written into the CFD cache, and then crashes the readers that DECISIONS 1033 says read a non-number as `inf`
severity: minor
lane: robustness
where: cafe/_seed.py:62-64 (`isinstance(g, (int, float))` and not bool: no finiteness or range test, by design for NaN and inf); cafe/pm/vv.py:165 (`gci_value`: `math.isfinite(g)` raises OverflowError for such an int); callers cafe/vehicle.py:290, :344, :398, cafe/hangar.py:253-255, cafe/mission.py via `vehicle`
reproduction: `t1.py`: a whole `quad.external.json` with every direction's `"gci": 10**400`: `main()` prints `seeded quad ('external', 'air', 10.0)`, exit 0, and the cache file is there. `t4b.py`: `vv.gci_value(10**400)` and `vv.gci_value(2**1100)` give `OverflowError: int too large to convert to float`; `hangar.grid_warning({"cfd": {"dirs": {"+x": {"gci": 10**400}}}})` the same. (NaN, inf, -inf, True, 'nan', None, a list, a Fraction, a Decimal, a numpy int or float32 are all `inf` as documented; a numpy float64 passes as its value; a missing key is 0.0, see the note under D-5.)
why it matters: 1033's contract is 'a number where finite, else `inf`, one function for every reader'; a hostile or damaged cached result is the case that contract is for, and here it ends in a crash of `vehicle.build` for that hull instead of the untrusted spread. The seed's check, written the same round, lets it in. Fix: `isfinite` guarded by a try for OverflowError (an int too large is not finite for this purpose), in `gci_value`, and `_seed` should call `vv.gci_value`'s notion of a number.
wrong data: no

### D-5. `mission.drag_sigma`: an infinite GCI is floored at 0.30, a huge finite one is not, and the lift GCI that drives it is printed nowhere
severity: minor
lane: physics / robustness
where: cafe/mission.py:70-72 (`finite` / `max(sig, SIG["drag_est"])`), cafe/mission.py:84-88 (`drag_sig = drag_sigma(v)` and `rng.normal(-drag_sig ** 2 / 2, drag_sig, B - 1)` in `Drone.__init__`); cafe/hangar.py:253-256 (`grid_warning` reads only the stored per-direction `gci`, not `limits['lift_gci']`)
reproduction: `t4b.py` on a stand-in vehicle (`drag_sigma` is a function of `v.drag`, `v.limits`, `v.sources`, `v.resistance` only): gci `[inf]`, `[inf, .1, .2]` and `[.1, .2, inf]` all give 0.30 (as 1033 says); finite 1.5 gives 1.503, finite 3.0 gives 3.002, 40 gives 40.0, 1e3 gives 1000.0, 1e200 gives 1e200. The sampled drag scale is `exp(N(-sigma^2/2, sigma))` (mean 1 by construction): median scale 0.32 at sigma 1.5 (61 % of members below 0.5), 0.011 at sigma 3 (90 % below 0.5), 0 beyond; at 1e200 `drag_sig ** 2` is `OverflowError: (34, 'Numerical result out of range')` in `Drone.__init__`, so a mission cannot be flown. A missing `gci` key reads 0.0 in `vehicle.py:290` and `hangar.py:253` (`grid_warning({"cfd": {"dirs": {"+x": {}}}})` is `None`), the opposite of an index that is NaN. The real results in the repo (`tests/fixtures/cfd_real`) have GCIs of 0.001 to 0.125, so sigma is 0.10 to 0.16 there; the 150 % SUBOFF value in `grid_warning`'s docstring is the pre-refinement one.
why it matters: the function was written so that 'no index' is never read as a small spread. A measured index of 3 (a grid study that is plainly not converged) gets a spread 10 times that of no index at all, and a mean-preserving lognormal that wide is not 'conservative' for a range-limited vehicle (the docstring of `grid_warning` says it is): the typical member flies with a few percent of the drag. The lift GCI (`hull_lift`, a difference of near-equal forces, so the largest of the inputs when it is) enters the max and is printed by no output, and `grid_warning` cannot see it. Nothing in the repo reaches it today; it is what a future torpedo or a coarse grid would reach first.
wrong data: yes, conditional on a result with a GCI above about 1 (the mission's verdicts for that vehicle); none in the repo's results or in q22
(suggestion: cap sigma at a stated bound with a warning, as `SIG["drag_est"]` is for a missing index; QUESTIONS_FOR_LAB 18h's alternative, refusing the vehicle, covers it too)
