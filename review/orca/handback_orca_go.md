### docs/ORCA_RUNNER.md: new section after "Stopping a job"

## The suite and the gate: one proot per job

Every traced system call of a job goes through one single-threaded `proot`. Measured on orca (`stat` calls per second, seccomp acceleration on): direct 1 process 389,000, 12 processes 1,525,000; one proot 44,000 for 1 process and 76,000 for 12; 12 separate proots 293,000. So 12 xdist workers under one proot got 1.7 times one worker's system-call rate, and 12 proots get 3.9 times that of one shared proot. The first full suite (12 workers, one proot, `bin/orca_suite.sh`) took 77.6 min and two timing tests failed from it. Real pipeline runs are little affected (their work is computing, not system calls); this is about test runs.

`bin/orca_gate.sh <K> <tag> [--cov]` (from the checkout; `PM_HOST` defaults to `cafe-orca`, `PMX` to `pm_<tag>`) syncs as `bin/pmx.sh` does and starts `bin/proot_suite.sh` detached in tmux session `<tag>` on orca (log `~/cafe-worker/logs/<tag>.log`, ending `rc=N`). `proot_suite.sh` runs K separate `proot_run.sh` jobs of 2 CPUs (K at most 16: 32 CPUs), each one plain pytest process (no xdist, `-vv`, `-m "not network"`) over its own share of the test files, all at once; then the real-time tests (`audit.SERIAL_TESTS`) alone. Shares come from `python -m cafe.pm.shares`: every test file in exactly one share, greedy by the file's time in the junit files of the tag's folder (`pmdata/shards/<tag>/`), or in the first suite's `pmdata/junit_91c9777.xml`, or by size. Reuse a tag and its last run balances the next. Each share logs to `~/cafe-worker/logs/<tag>_s<i>.log` (a failure has its name while it runs), the serial tests to `<tag>_serial.log`, each ending `rc=N`. A job passes only if its exit status and its junit file agree.

With `--cov` each job writes its own coverage file, then `coverage combine`, `coverage json` to `/app/coverage.json`, and `python -m cafe.pm.audit --pytest-done` (ruff, mypy, the scans, the mutation record and the coverage gates; it fails if `coverage.json` is missing or older than the newest file under `cafe/pm`). `proot_suite.sh` removes an old `coverage.json` first (`bin/sync_filters` keeps it across syncs). The gate exits 0 only if every job and the audit passed, and names what failed (`FAILED: s2(rc=1) audit(rc=1)`). Stop it like any job: `tmux kill-session -t <tag>` (or TERM, INT, HUP to `proot_suite.sh`): every job gets TERM through its `proot_run.sh`. Check `/proc/loadavg` first: the sum of CPUs over everything of ours must stay at 32 or below, and with K above 8 the first minutes of a run (imports, thread pools sized to orca's 64 CPUs) can reach it.

Measured (K=8, 2026-11-06, about 3,260 tests): 35 to 37 min without coverage (the same suite took 77.6 min as 12 xdist workers under one proot); 54 min with `--cov` (coverage doubles `tests/test_pipeline.py`, so reuse a tag that was itself run with `--cov` to balance a coverage run). The floor is `tests/test_stage3.py` alone, about 34 min, so K above 5 or 6 helps nothing and K=8 is a safe choice. A share costs about 10 s to start (imports, collection, the session fixtures of `tests/conftest.py`).

### docs/ORCA_RUNNER.md, "What is not the same as docker": add

- Exit status. proot answers with the status of the last process of the job to end, not of the first (`sh -c "(sleep 3; exit 7) & exit 1"` gave 7), and a child that outlives the job, such as the resource tracker `multiprocessing` starts for spawned workers, exits 0: a failed pytest or pipeline run read `rc=0`. `bin/proot_run.sh` now runs the job inside a small `sh -c` that writes its status to the job's `/tmp` and exits with that (a job killed outright writes none, and then proot's status stands). `rc=N` in a log is the job's own, as with docker.
- Two tests of `tests/test_runners.py` (the rsync sync one) need `--no-perms` under proot on kernel 5.10: rsync's chmod fails there ("failed to set permissions ... No such file or directory"). The tests do it themselves.
