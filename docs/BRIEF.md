1.4 What the dataset means physically (so the demo mission is realistic)

* March 2023 = MJO / westerly-wind-burst at the eastern edge of the warm pool during 2023 El Niño onset (the TEPEX-C season and region). Expect WWB 3–9 m/s, warm-pool edge advancing ~0.8 m/s, Yoshida jet ~1 m/s at the surface, SEC 0.25–0.75 m/s westward, EUC core 0.5–1.5 m/s at ~100–140 m depth. Gliders at 0.25–0.4 m/s cannot hold station against the EUC or a jet; Saildrones (~1.4 m/s, hull-speed cap ~3.3 m/s) have historically failed in <0.5 m/s wind with >0.9 m/s current (2018 TPOS mission ended early for exactly this). The demo mission must exercise these failure modes, not avoid them.
* Hs ~1–4 m, swell-dominated, dominant periods 8–18 s. Diurnal warm layers of 0.5–3 °C under <3–4 m/s wind are expected in reality and absent from this WRF run.

2.2 Sub-grid atmosphere and ocean (replaces "L2 moving LES nests" as the default path)
Rationale: the 12 km/1 h forcing has nothing between 12 km and the vehicle. An LES nest fed by 12 km fields spends its first 10–30 km developing turbulence from nothing (cell-perturbation method, Muñoz-Esparza 2014/2018; Mazzaro 2017), which is most of the nest for a 7 m boat. Decision, not an option: the stochastic/synthetic sub-grid layer below is what Stage 1 uses on every machine; the L2 moving LES nest (§2.6) is also built, runs on every GPU budget, and its job is to validate and recalibrate the synthetic layer (compare resolved spectra, σ_u/u*, length scales, and gust statistics in each worst-moment window; write the calibration back to `config/subgrid.json`). On CPU-only runs the last written calibration is used and the report says which run produced it.

* Wind: Mann or Kaimal/IEC-61400-1 synthetic turbulence box (use `mannrs` or `PyConTurb`/`PyTurbSim`; do not write your own spectral generator) with σ_u = 2.5 u*, length scales from z and z/L, frozen-turbulence advection at the WRF mean wind, regenerated per Monte-Carlo member with its own seed. Add a gust-front model: with probability tied to WRF `RAINNC` rate and CAPE-like proxies, inject a cold-pool event (Δwind +3 to +10 m/s, ΔT −1.5 °C, 10–30 min) following Wills et al. 2021 statistics for the tropical Pacific.
* Ocean: Lagrangian stochastic velocity perturbation (Itô SDE with `∂K/∂x` drift term, Euler–Maruyama; `OceanParcels` diffusion tutorial formulation) on top of HYCOM, plus Stokes drift from the synthesized spectrum (Breivik 2016 profile; do not use single-exponential), plus windage 1.8–2.3 % for surface vehicles. Add the diurnal warm-layer model (Fairall 1996 / COARE warm-layer) driven by WRF `SWDOWN` and `U10`, enabled by default since WRF itself has none; it changes near-surface density, the afternoon surface-trapped jet proxy (add +0.1–0.3 m/s downwind surface current scaled by ΔSST, labelled as modelled; it is TEPEX-C's own hypothesis), and glider surfacing thermistors.
* Rain fresh lenses: implement (Iyer & Drushka 2021: top ~0.5 m, ΔS ≈ 0.5 psu, ~7 km, persist minutes–hours below ~5 m/s wind, absent above ~8 m/s), triggered by WRF `RAINNC` rate; it alters near-surface density for glider ballast at surfacing and the USV's SST/SSS sensor exposure. Ships enabled with a config switch and a unit test; it is small and the lab's science (barrier layers) cares about it.

2.3 Wave realizations from integrated parameters (new; required because the spectrum is absent)

* Parametric directional spectrum: Donelan–Hamilton–Hui 1985 (preferred for wind sea; it is UMWM's own lineage) or JONSWAP with γ fitted so that Tp = `dwp`, Hs = `swh`, plus a swell partition when `dwp` ≫ wind-sea peak from `wspd` (Pierson–Moskowitz fetch-unlimited check); `cos^{2s}` spreading with s from `mss` (fit s so the spectral mean-square slope matches the file's `mss`). Directional convention per §1.2.

* Expose `η(x,y,t)`, orbital `u,v,w(z)`, slope, and encounter-frequency spectra to the 6-DOF model and to Capytaine/Cummins.
* Unit tests: Hs reproduced within 1 %; spectral moments m0, m2 match; Stokes surface drift matches `2 m3/g`; a 1-h realization has no visible repeat (autocorrelation test).

2.4 Vehicle models that match what TEPEX-C will actually fly
The plan's platforms are "UUV, USV, UAV/UAS"; nothing in the public plan names models. Build three parameterized classes with published defaults and a `platforms/*.yaml` that the lab fills (§7):

* Underwater glider (Seaglider/Slocum class): keep the built-in controller. ArduSub's `SIM_Submarine` is thruster-only — no buoyancy engine, fins or mass-shifter outputs exist — so ArduSub can only be an EKF/mission consumer for a glider, never its controller. Use the UW/APL Seaglider flight model (Bennett, Stahr & Eriksen 2019 basestation FlightModel; Frajka-Williams et al. 2011): steady balance `L + B cosθ − D sinθ = 0`, `L = l²·a·q·α`, `D = l²·q^(1+s)·(b + c·α²)`, `B = g(ρV − M)`, `V = (V₀ + ΔV_vbd)·exp(−γp + α_T(T−T₀))`, `l = 1.8 m`. Ship the published defaults as the yaml: Seaglider `hd_a = 0.003548, hd_b = 0.011220, hd_c = 5.7e-6, hd_s = −0.25, abs_compress = 4.10e-6, therm_expan = 7.05e-5`, volmax ≈ 51 000 cc, 1000 m, ~0.2–0.5 m/s path speed, glide slope 1:1–1:5, pitch by battery shift, Iridium ~200 B/s at surfacing. For Slocum use the Merckelbach 2010/2019 form (`gliderflight` Python package is the oracle: params mg, Vg, Cd0, Cd1, ah, epsilon; Table-1 defaults S = 0.10 m², AR = 7, a_w = 3.7 rad⁻¹, C_D0 ≈ 0.10, m_g 56.4 kg, V_g 54.8 L). Receipt: steady glide vs the published flight-model relations and vs `gliderflight` on identical inputs.
* Wing-sail USV (Saildrone Explorer class): 7 m hull, 5 m rigid wing, 2 m draft, keel 2.5 m, wing anemometer at 5.2 m; average 1–3 kn; the only published performance numbers are Chiodi et al. 2021 (mean SOG 0.96 m/s ≈ 18 % of a 5.4 m/s mean wind; max hourly 2.9 m/s at 12 m/s) and Mazza/Zhang/Chiodi/Chen 2026 Part 1 (3-DOF simulator, MCMC-calibrated on SD-1030, RK4 at 0.2 s, ~1.4 m/s typical, 3.3 m/s hull-speed cap). Added resistance in waves via Gerritsma–Beukelman from the Capytaine RAOs. Receipt: reproduce the Chiodi 2021 SOG-vs-wind band and the Zhang 2019 SPURS-2 failure (swept off track at 0.9 m/s current / 0.2 m/s wind). Failure modes to add: becalmed (SOG < current for > N h), broach/capsize proxy from roll RAO + wind heeling moment, wing stall.
* Small quadrotor UAS: keep ArduCopter SITL through the JSON external-physics backend (UDP 9002/9003, `-I N` adds 10·N to the sim ports, `SIM_RATE_HZ` default 1200, lockstep driven by your monotonically increasing `timestamp`; reply JSON needs `timestamp, imu.gyro, imu.accel_body, velocity[NED]` and `attitude` or `quaternion`). `SIM_JSON.cpp` states wind is not supported for JSON vehicles — `SIM_WIND_*` is ignored — so all wind, turbulence, and gust-front effects are applied in your physics: rotor-drag / blade-flapping model (Faessler, Franchi, Scaramuzza 2018: `v̇ = −g z_W + c z_B − R D Rᵀ v`, D = diag(d_x,d_y,d_z) identified from NeuroBEM or Blackbird data), identified propulsion curve, Peukert battery (P ≈ 1.05). Physics step ≤ 2.5 ms as the spec says, implemented multirate (physics 2.5 ms, environment lookup 50 ms). Do not use `SIM_SPEEDUP` with the external backend; parallelize instances instead.
* Keep the Petrel/Tern/Gnat examples as the test fixtures; the three yaml defaults above become the demo mission's vehicles.

2.5 Monte Carlo, statistics and verdicts (upgrade Stage 1; this is what the lab reads)

* Move the 6-DOF ensemble to `jax.vmap` + `diffrax` (or keep NumPy but vectorized over members) so 256 members of a 7-day mission run in minutes on CPU. SITL vehicles stay serial: run them in parallel instances (`-I n`), cap them, and state in the report how many SITL members were run versus the pure-physics ensemble.
* Sampling: Latin-hypercube over the perturbation parameters (wind bias/scale, current bias/rotation, Hs scale, propulsion efficiency, battery capacity, drag-coefficient uncertainty from GCI), common random numbers across vehicles in the same member so inter-vehicle range checks are consistent, antithetic pairs on the dominant uncertainties.
* Default perturbation magnitudes (make them config, cite them in the credibility report, mark as priors): wind speed σ = 2.0 m/s (coastal/tropical WRF RMSE 1.8–2.5 m/s), direction σ = 15°; current speed ±30 % with correlation length 100 km, direction σ = 20° (HYCOM-vs-drifter speed correlation ≈ 0.5 in strong-current regimes); Hs ±15 % (WW3-class NRMSE 10–20 % in the equatorial Pacific), Tp ±1 s; bulk stress ±30 %; glider speed-through-water ±10 %; USV VPP bias −30 % (still-water VPPs overpredict SOG by 30–35 % in trials).
* Verdicts: keep Wilson intervals (Brown, Cai & DasGupta 2001 recommend Wilson for n ≤ 40, Agresti–Coull above). Minimum ensemble: with zero failures the two-sided 95 % Wilson upper bound is κ²/(n+κ²), so n = 73 is the smallest ensemble that can certify P(fail) < 5 % (rule of three gives n = 59 one-sided); the report must say "P(fail) < 3/n" when it has fewer. Add SPRT-style sequential stopping (Wald; Younes & Simmons indifference region) so a vehicle's ensemble stops when the CI is entirely inside GO or NO-GO, and subset simulation (Au & Beck 2001) for rare failures only if a vehicle lands MARGINAL after the base ensemble.
* Each verdict must name: the dominant failure mode, the first time it occurs, the perturbation parameter it is most sensitive to (Sobol first-order from the LHS sample is enough), and the smallest change in the mission that flips it (deploy 6 h later, 0.5° west, lower duty cycle) found by a cheap local search on the emulator (§2.7).

2.6 High-resolution windows: LES + FEA only where Stage 1 says it matters (replaces "L2/L3 everywhere")

* From Stage 1, pick the K worst windows per vehicle (max |wind|, max Hs at encounter, max current, max structural load proxy). Only those windows get an L3 near-body LES and an FEA pass.
* Solver: lattice-Boltzmann on the GPU. Default XLB (Apache-2.0) with the Warp backend (≈95 % of FluidX3D speed; the JAX backend is ~8× slower and 2–3× more memory, kept only as the CPU-fallback/test backend). FluidX3D is implemented as a second backend behind the same interface (it is faster and has free-surface VoF) but ships disabled: its license forbids commercial and military use and requires publishing modifications, so it is a plugin the lab enables with one config line after they read `docs/LICENSES.md`. Both backends must pass the same §5.1 LBM tests; CI runs XLB-Warp on GPU when present and XLB-JAX on CPU always. OpenFOAM stays CPU-only for the Hangar (GPU OpenFOAM is solver-offload only and ≤1× at 1–5 M cells in 2026).
* Resolution rule, written into `planner.py`: wall-modelled LES with 5–10 cells per boundary-layer thickness and an equilibrium wall model (κ = 0.41, B = 5.2; matching point at the 3rd cell), Smagorinsky Cs ≈ 0.17 or WALE Cw = 0.325. For a 1.5 m hull at 1.5 m/s (Re ≈ 2×10⁶, δ_TE ≈ 30 mm) that is Δ ≈ 3 mm, ≈ 20 M cells in a 2×0.5×0.5 m box, 10 s physical ≈ 10⁵ LBM steps ≈ 2–3 min on an RTX PRO 6000/5090. Wall-resolved LES (10⁹ cells) is out of scope; say so in the report when a user asks for it.
* Moving-nest machinery: implement GO-MELT-style fixed-size arrays with whole-cell shifts (`jnp.roll` + boundary refill), coarse→fine trilinear Dirichlet inflow from the L1+§2.2 field, fine→coarse residual forcing, vmapped over vehicles. Use XLB's Neon nested-cuboid refinement for the 2:1 levels rather than writing your own. Keep the nest one-way; document that two-way feedback into WRF/HYCOM is not attempted.
* Free-surface boat windows: interFoam on CPU at Δ ≈ 1 cm with the two-grid GCI the Hangar already does is the default (30–60 min per window; the planner budgets for it and runs it concurrently with GPU work); FluidX3D FSLBM replaces it automatically when the plugin is enabled. Both paths must reproduce the DTMB 5415 C_T points in §5.2 within their GCI band.
* FEA: CalculiX with `OMP_NUM_THREADS=8`, PaStiX if built; loads from the LES window (pressure field mapped onto the FEA mesh) and from the slamming pressure estimate; check hull collapse (Bresse Receipt), wing/arm root stress, boat slamming. Output a structural utilization ratio per window.
* Refit: from each window, recompute the vehicle's drag/lift/added-resistance coefficients and their spread, push them back as a Stage-1 perturbation distribution, rerun Stage 1 (this is the "refit the drone's coefficients" step in the spec; make it a loop with a convergence check on the verdicts, max 2 iterations).

2.7 Emulator (what makes mission-time evaluation cheap)

* Scope: the emulator maps (mission parameters, environment summary statistics along the track, vehicle parameters) → (P(fail), time-to-fail, margin per failure mode). It is trained on Stage-1 ensembles the tool has already run (every run appends to `emulator/train.parquet`), never on synthetic data alone.
* Model: Gaussian-process regression (SMT `KRG`/`KPLS` or GPyTorch) with a multi-fidelity option (Kennedy–O'Hagan co-kriging) once ≥ 3 L3-refit runs exist; conformal prediction on top so predictive intervals have guaranteed coverage (report empirical coverage at 90 % nominal on a held-out 20 %).
* Use: (a) the "smallest change that flips the verdict" search in §2.5, (b) a `--budget quick` mode that returns emulator verdicts with "EMULATED" badges and the training-set distance, (c) active learning: pick the next full run where the GP variance is highest among candidate missions the lab lists.
* Acceptance: held-out RMSE on P(fail) ≤ 0.05, interval coverage within ±5 % of nominal, query time < 10 ms, and a Receipt that fails if coverage drops.

2.8 Communications and range checks (extend the existing range verdicts)

* RF: two-ray over a smooth sea `L(d) = −20 log10[(λ/4πd)·2 sin(2π h_t h_r/(λd))]` plus a 4/3-earth radio horizon, antenna heights from the platform yaml; link budget from radio yaml (default RFD900x: 902–928 MHz, +30 dBm, −121 dBm sensitivity at low rate). Sea-state loss: add a roughness term scaled by the local `swh` and flag it as modelled. Iridium SBD (340 B MO / 270 B MT) and RUDICS modelled as latency + availability, not range.
* Acoustic: Thorp absorption `10 log a(f) = 0.11f²/(1+f²) + 44f²/(4100+f²) + 2.75e-4 f² + 0.003` dB/km (f in kHz), path loss `A = l^k a(f)^l` with k = 1.5 default, noise from the Stojanovic turbulence/shipping/wind/thermal terms using WRF wind, sound-speed profile from TEOS-10 on the GOFS T/S column. Bellhop ray tracing (`aubellhop`/`arlpy`) is implemented as the high-fidelity path and runs automatically for every acoustic link whose Thorp-budget margin is within ±6 dB of the threshold (the ambiguous cases); the Thorp budget alone decides the clear cases. Receipt: Bellhop vs Thorp agree within 3 dB on an isovelocity profile. Modem yaml defaults: Teledyne Benthos ATM-960 LF 9–14 kHz ≥ 6 km, MF 16–21 kHz ≥ 4 km, HF 22–27 kHz ≥ 2 km; WHOI Micro-Modem 10 kHz long-range / 25 kHz short-range bands with no published km figure (so the range comes from the budget, not a table).
* Verdict "can the pairs that must talk stay in range" now uses the Monte-Carlo positions, not nominal tracks.

2.9 `run_premission` orchestration, checkpoints, report

* Stages: `ingest → hangar(cached) → stage1 → windows → stage3 → refit → stage1' → emulator-update → report`. Each stage writes a checkpoint and a ledger (§5.5); `--resume` continues from the last good ledger; `--budget {ci,quick,4h,overnight}` sets ensemble size, K windows, L2/L3 resolution and backend via `planner.py` from the measured throughput of this machine (`bench/` benchmarks OpenFOAM on CPU, XLB-Warp on GPU, XLB-JAX on CPU, 6-DOF members/s, and stores `config/throughput.json`). `ci` runs every stage at toy resolution on CPU in < 10 min and is what CI executes on every commit.
* Companion commands, all sharing the same engine: `cafe fetch` (data, resumable, hash-checked, bounding-box aware), `cafe validate` (schema + convention checks on any file), `cafe hangar` (characterize one vehicle), `cafe whatif --mission … --shift 48h | --move 1deg W | --drop glider_2` (emulator-first, falls back to Stage 1), `cafe receipts`, `cafe audit`, `cafe report --open`.
* One report per mission: GO/MARGINAL/NO-GO per vehicle and per link, a map (track + reach map + where the failures happen), the top-3 sensitivities, the "what would fix it" line, the environment summary along the track with the data source for each field, the credibility table (§5.4), the list of assumptions, and the raw `report.json`.
* The web UI replays stay; the static demo stays; both read `report.json`.

What the planner should know about GPU tiers (published numbers; the lab's own `bench/` run overrides them):

* Lattice-Boltzmann is memory-bandwidth-bound: throughput ≈ bandwidth / bytes-moved-per-cell-update. FluidX3D measured: RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB, 1.8 TB/s) 20.8 GLUPS; RTX 5090 (32 GB) 19.1; H100 SXM 29.6 (FP16S); A100 SXM 18.4; DGX Spark 2.4; M3 Ultra 8.2. * Translation: "< 1 h on 8×H100" ≈ 8–12 h on one PRO 6000 at the same cell counts, or < 1 h with nests halved per axis. On a CPU-only box the LES windows are replaced by the Hangar RANS coefficients with their GCI spread and the report says so.
* Not viable anywhere: Apple Silicon for the JAX path (jax-metal abandoned); GPU OpenFOAM at 1–5 M cells (solver-offload only, ≤ 1×); wall-resolved hull LES (10⁹ cells).
* Recommended spec table for `docs/RUNBOOK.md` (so the lab can check their server against it): one RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell (96 GB), 32–64 cores, 256 GB RAM, 4 TB NVMe is the comfortable tier; an RTX 5090 does the same job with ≤ 4 nests at 256³ and K ≤ 3 windows per vehicle; any 24 GB card still runs everything at halved nest resolution; CPU-only runs Stage 1 + Hangar + emulator fully and labels the LES-derived rows.

Optimizations that are required, not optional:

* Hangar results cached by (CAD hash, mesh params, solver version); never rerun for the same vehicle.
* Environment subsets cached as Zarr; all interpolation vectorized; no per-member file I/O.
* Stage-1 ensemble vectorized over members (vmap or NumPy batch); SITL members capped and parallel.
* LBM in FP32 compute / FP16 storage (DDF-shifting keeps 4th-digit accuracy) with a `--fp32-storage` switch for the Receipts run.
* LES windows run sequentially on one GPU; FEA runs on CPU concurrently with the next LES window.
* Benchmark harness: `bench/` records cell-updates/s for OpenFOAM (CPU), XLB-Warp (GPU), 6-DOF members/s, and feeds `planner.py`; the report prints the measured numbers it planned against.
* Memory guard: planner refuses a configuration that would exceed 85 % of GPU memory and says what to reduce.

5. Verification that the whole thing works (new Receipts and gates; all must be able to fail)
5.1 Code verification

* 6-DOF integrator: free rigid body must conserve |H| and kinetic energy (symplectic/semi-implicit Euler keeps a bounded modified energy; explicit Euler grows — the test must distinguish them); quaternion-norm drift bound; MMS on a forced trajectory; compare against Fossen's `PythonVehicleSimulator` `remus100` and `otter` on identical inputs (those are the oracles for the AUV and USV hull terms).
* Zermelo planner: uniform current ⇒ constant heading, straight line; linear shear `v_x = −Vy/h` ⇒ `d(tanθ)/dt = V/h`; solid-body rotation ⇒ θ̇ = ω. All three as unit tests with analytic tolerances.
* LBM: Poiseuille and lid-driven cavity against reference; Taylor–Green decay rate; sphere drag vs the Morrison 2013 correlation at Re = 10², 10³, 10⁴ within 10 %; flat-plate C_f vs ITTC-57 `0.075/(log10 Re − 2)²` with the wall model.
* Nest: a vehicle-following nest in a uniform flow must produce zero spurious forces; a nest crossing a shear layer must conserve mass to < 0.1 % per step.
* GCI (Celik et al. 2008 procedure: r ≥ 1.3, observed order p, Richardson extrapolate, `GCI_fine = 1.25 e_a/(r^p − 1)`) on every Hangar case; ASME V&V 20 tabulation (E = S − D, U_num, U_input, U_D, U_val) for every validation case below — validation is reported, not pass/fail.

5.2 Model validation (existing + new)

* Existing: Wigley/Kajitani, REMUS/Prestero, SUBOFF/Roddy, Bresse.
* New: DTMB 5415 (INSEAN 2340, Lpp 5.72 m) total resistance C_T = 4.02e-3 at Fr 0.10, 3.95e-3 at 0.20, 4.23e-3 at 0.28, 6.67e-3 at 0.41 for the free-surface path; Seaglider steady-glide relations and `gliderflight` cross-check; Saildrone SOG-vs-wind band (Chiodi 2021) and the SD-1030-calibrated behaviour from Mazza et al. 2026; quadrotor rotor-drag identification against NeuroBEM (0.772 kg, J = diag(0.0025, 0.0021, 0.0043), 400 Hz) or Blackbird; COARE 3.5 `u*`/`C_D` vs the Edson 2013 curve; Stokes drift vs Breivik 2016's Phillips-spectrum closed form; synthesized Hs vs file `swh`; UMWM `ust` vs COARE `u*`.
* Data validation (mandatory, automated): WRF 10 m wind and SST along the TAO/TRITON moorings inside the domain for March 2023 vs the buoys (PMEL TAO data delivery, `https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/tao/drupal/disdel/` or the OceanSITES/ERDDAP mirror; script the download, cache it, and check the hash) — report RMSE, bias and direction error per mooring; the fitted numbers replace the literature wind prior in §2.5 and the SST comparison quantifies the frozen-SST error. 5.5 Checkpoint ledger and `make audit` (every stage proves itself)
Every stage of `run_premission` writes `checkpoints/<stage>/ledger.json` containing: input file paths + SHA-256, config hash, code git SHA, library versions, random seeds, wall-time, machine fingerprint (`bench/` id), the list of self-checks it ran with pass/fail and the numbers, and the SHA-256 of every output. Self-checks per stage, all mandatory, all able to fail:

* `ingest`: files parsed match the §1 schema exactly (dims, units, conventions) — a schema file in `cafe/schemas/` is the contract and the test; time axis monotonic and gap-free across file boundaries; no NaN in sea cells; `swh` from synthesized spectrum vs file `swh` ≤ 1 %; COARE `u*` vs UMWM `ust` agreement logged; GOFS temperature flagged in-situ; direction-convention round-trip test on three fields; bounding box covers every waypoint ± 2° or the stage refuses.
* `hangar`: GCI computed, observed order 1.5 ≤ p ≤ 3 or flagged; force coefficients within the Receipt band for every reference case; mesh quality stats recorded; cache key recorded.
* `stage1`: ensemble size ≥ 73 unless `--budget quick`, in which case the report says "cannot certify < 5 %"; LHS stratification verified (marginal uniformity test); CRN verified (same seed → identical environment draws across vehicles); energy/|H| drift of every member below threshold; every failure mode exercised at least once by the unit fixtures; Wilson interval arithmetic cross-checked against `statsmodels`.
* `windows`: chosen windows are the argmax of the stated criteria (assert it); window spans and the reason recorded.
* `stage3`: LBM Mach ≤ 0.1 and Courant checks; wall-model matching point in the log layer (y⁺ in 30–300) at ≥ 90 % of wall cells; force time-series converged (running mean within 2 % over the last third); mass conservation ≤ 0.1 %; nest shift count and interpolation residual recorded; FEA max utilization and element quality recorded.
* `refit`: coefficient change vs Hangar value and its spread recorded; verdict changes between stage1 and stage1′ listed; loop converged or capped at 2 with the reason.
* `emulator`: training-set size, held-out RMSE, coverage at 90 %, nearest-neighbour distance of the queried mission; refuses to answer (returns "out of range") when the distance exceeds a calibrated threshold.
* `report`: every number in `report.json` is traceable to a ledger entry (a test walks the JSON and asserts the back-link exists); the HTML and JSON agree. `make audit` re-reads all ledgers, re-hashes every input and output, asserts the chain is unbroken, asserts zero failed self-checks, greps the repo for `TODO|FIXME|XXX|NotImplementedError|placeholder|skip\(|xfail` and fails on any hit outside `tests/fixtures/broken/`, runs `ruff`, `mypy --strict` on `cafe/`, and `pytest --cov` with ≥ 90 % line coverage on `cafe/` (100 % on `cafe/physics/`, `cafe/stats/`, `cafe/env/`). Also run `mutmut` (or `cosmic-ray`) on `cafe/physics/` and `cafe/stats/` and require ≥ 85 % mutants killed — a sign flip in a force term must be caught by a test. Property-based tests (`hypothesis`) for every unit conversion, every coordinate/direction transform, and every interpolation (bounds, monotonicity, symmetry).

5.6 Self-adversarial loop (you do not get to declare "done")
