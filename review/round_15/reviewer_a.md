### A-1. `stage1._link_hs` takes the larger Hs of the two ends "because it is the conservative one"; for the two-ray link the larger Hs gives the larger worst-case margin, so it is the optimistic end
severity: minor
lane: physics / documentation
where: cafe/pm/stage1.py:262-275 (docstring "The larger is the conservative one", `np.maximum(*both)`); DECISIONS.md row 651 ("the larger is the conservative one (a rougher sea lowers the reflected ray's strength)"); cafe/pm/physics/comms.py:145-147 (the repo's own docstring shows the same effect)
reproduction: on orca, `python rev15a_p1.py` (scratch, removed): `comms.rf_margin(d, ht, hr, hs, "rfd900x")` for d = 0.5 to 40 km (4000 points), ht 15 m (the demo's ship antenna), hr 60 m. Minimum margin over d <= 15 km: Hs 0.0 m: 3.1 dB; 0.2 m: 3.5; 0.5: 5.3; 1: 9.8; 2: 18.0; 3: 24.0; 5: 31.8. With hr 120 m: -0.0 dB (0.999 of the points above 0), 0.2, 1.3, 4.5, 11.4, 17.0, 24.5. The median margin does not move (46.0 to 45.9 dB; 43.0 to 43.6). A rougher sea weakens the reflected ray, which fills the interference nulls: the lowest margin rises with Hs. `comms.py:145-147` shows the same: the curved/plane-earth disagreement at the nulls, which measures how sharp they are, falls from 20 dB at Hs 0 to 2.4 dB at Hs 1 m and 0.3 dB at 3 m. So `max(Hs_a, Hs_b)` picks, of the two ends, the one that gives the higher worst-case margin; the conservative end is the smaller Hs (`np.minimum`), and neither end is the right one (the reflection point lies between them). The mutant `np.maximum` to `np.minimum` is killed by `tests/test_stage1.py` (they pin the symmetry, not the reason).
why it matters: the link margin, `frac` and the lost-link time of a mission with a calm sea at one end and a rough one at the other (Hs 0.2 m at the ship, 3 m at the USV: the case the row quotes) are made optimistic by the choice, by up to about 20 dB in a null, under a sentence that says the opposite; the acoustic branch (larger wind, more noise) is right.

### A-7. `hangar.bem` evaluates roll and pitch added mass at the heave frequency while its comment says "near the natural frequencies of heave/roll/pitch"
severity: minor
lane: physics
where: cafe/hangar.py:110-112 (`w_heave = np.sqrt(9.81 / max(draft, 0.02))`; `w = w_heave if d in ("Heave", "Roll", "Pitch") else 0.3`)
reproduction: by reading: one frequency, sqrt(g / draft) (the heave frequency of a wall-sided hull with no added mass), is used for the three dofs; a monohull's roll frequency is sqrt(g GM / k_roll^2) and its pitch frequency follows its own waterplane inertia, and neither equals the heave one (for a 0.145 m draft the heave value is 8.2 rad/s; a roll period of a few seconds is 1 to 3 rad/s). Not run (Capytaine was not started): the size of the difference in `added_mass[Roll]`/`damping` is not measured. The pm pipeline's hangar stage does not call `bem` (only `receipts.py:338` for the UUV, where the frequency is not used), so only the live app's USV Hangar is affected.
why it matters: the radiation damping and added inertia of the roll and pitch rows of the app's USV Hangar are for a frequency the hull does not oscillate at in those dofs, under a comment that says it is.
