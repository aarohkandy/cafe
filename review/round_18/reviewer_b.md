## Findings

### B-1. The sensitivity quantity is not the dominant failure mode's for the USV (battery) and for a glider on station (battery), so the card names a parameter that does not drive the failure and can never name the one that does
severity: minor
lane: statistics, honesty
where: cafe/pm/stage1.py:815 (`key = "profile_margin" if dom == "profile_incomplete" ... else "margin"`: only the UAS gets the margin of its dominant mode), :52-55 (`SENS_ACTS`: `solar_yield_scale` left out of the USV's columns, DECISIONS 923), :147-171 (`_run_usv` stores `energy_margin`, the lowest state of charge; the sensitivity reads it nowhere), `_run_glider` :122-145 (station: `margin` is the maximum distance from the station)
reproduction: `x_b/usvbat.py` on orca (`stage1._run_usv` with the CI fixture's USV, `AnalyticEnv(wind=(-6,0), swdown=even)` where `even` = hotel / (solar_eff area), `soc0` 0.07, 24 h, 48 members from `sampling.lhs` over the real priors): 10 of 48 members fail, all of them by `battery`. `_sensitivity(names, U, f, set(SENS_ACTS['usv']), r['margin'], r['margin_name'])` (what `summarize` calls) prints: "usv_vpp_bias has the largest first-order index (S1 0.997) for the maximum cross-track error, above the 0.253 that unrelated outcomes reach". With the state of charge the same members give `solar_yield_scale` S1 0.977 (floor 0.254) and `usv_vpp_bias` 0.16. For a station glider whose dominant mode is `battery`, the probe shows `battery_capacity_scale` moves no value of `margin` (maximum distance from the station) at all (`x_b/sens.py`, glider_eq: the column's row is empty), so the table cannot rank the one column that decides a battery failure there either.
why it matters: the card's "most sensitive to" line for a vehicle whose verdict comes from its battery points at the steering prior, and the column that decides the failure is excluded by DECISIONS 923's own argument (the margin "is the cross-track error"), which holds only for the margin, not for the failure the verdict is about. Round 16's M-10 stored the lowest state of charge for exactly this.
wrong data: yes, for a mission whose USV (or station glider) fails mainly by battery: the "most sensitive to" text of that vehicle. The q18 demo is not affected (USV battery 0 of 64, glider_eq's mode is `station_lost`).

### B-2. An RF link between two low antennas (two gliders) has its loss overstated by 6 to 11 dB or more, outside the stated "about 5 dB"; the loader allows such a link
severity: minor
lane: physics, documentation
where: cafe/pm/physics/comms.py:130-141 (the low-antenna band: measured with a 15 m partner only), cafe/pm/credibility.py:83 (L23: "about 5 dB at the Slocum's 0.3 m"), docs/PHYSICS.md:809, DECISIONS 926; cafe/pm/config.py:495-505 (an `rf` link with `when: surfacing` and a glider at one or both ends is accepted: nothing refuses two gliders)
reproduction: `x_b/gg.py` (exact flat-earth Fresnel two-ray with eps 72.8 + j118.9 against `two_ray_loss_db`, Hs 0, 915 MHz, 200 m to the radio horizon): repo minus Fresnel, min / median / max dB: Seaglider 0.5 m with Slocum 0.3 m **+9.0 / +9.3 / +9.3**; two Slocums 0.3 / 0.3 m **+11.1 / +11.4 / +11.4**; two Seagliders 0.5 / 0.5 m +6.3 / +6.7 / +6.7. The flat-earth Fresnel has no surface wave, so these are lower bounds of the overstatement (the field goes as h1 h2 with R = -1, so the two heights' errors compound). `rf_margin(d, 0.5, 0.3, 0.5)` prints 27.6, 15.5, 3.5 and -8.6 dB at 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 km; the Fresnel loss is 9 dB lower at each, i.e. a link needed at 4 km shows NO-GO (-8.6) where a 9 dB lower loss gives about +0.7. (A PE with a 0.5 m source was tried and not used: it did not reproduce the Fresnel field for a source below about 1 m, +-5 to 20 dB with the beam width, so only the Fresnel numbers are claimed.) With a 2.2 m partner the PE says +3.7 dB median for 0.3 m (inside the band), so the band is right for a glider-USV link and wrong for a glider-glider one.
why it matters: the credibility page tells the user the shortfall is "about 5 dB" and "a GO can show as MARGINAL"; for two low antennas it is 9 to 11 dB and a GO can show as NO-GO.
wrong data: yes, for a mission with an RF link whose two ends are gliders: that link's margin and verdict (conservative direction). Not in the demo.

### B-3. DECISIONS 926, PHYSICS 12.1 and L23 say the RF margin is "too low, never too high" and that the shipped USV antenna is "not affected"; the same rows' own figures and the PE show it too high for 0.5 m and 2.2 m

severity: minor
lane: statistics
