# DECISIONS — where cafe keeps its existing choice, or deviates from the spec

One line of why each. HR = hard requirement in the spec.

| # | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Build and measure on homebase CPU (8 vCPU, no GPU); the compute backend is a config switch (`cpu` / `gpu`). | No GPU exists and Azure GPU quota is 0; HR "1 h on 8×H100" can't be verified until the quota request lands. |
| 2 | L1 stays WRF + HYCOM + WW3 as read today (`env.py`, `ocean.py`). | It is the existing, already-run environment model; one-way and read-only as the HR wants. |
| 3 | The existing Monte Carlo (`mission.run`) is stage 1, the ensemble — not rewritten. | Already Fossen 6-DOF, relative velocity, batched, with Wilson intervals and receipts. |
| 4 | Keep semi-implicit Euler instead of RK4. | The spec allows semi-implicit, and it is what the HR asks for with added mass; receipts pass on it. |
| 5 | Moving nests follow GO-MELT (Leonor & Wagner): fixed-size arrays per level that move with their source by whole cells, no remeshing, JIT kernels. | Matches the spec's HR "whole-cell shifts only", and fixed shapes are what let JAX compile once and run the same code on CPU or GPU. |
| 6 | Nest-solver choice (OpenFOAM vs a JAX lattice-Boltzmann LES) stays open until both are benchmarked; the planner takes per-solver throughput. | OpenFOAM is the solver in use (HR: benchmark the actual solver) but has no production GPU path; LBM is the candidate that does. |
| 7 | Quad dynamics step is 2.5 ms (was 10 ms), the other vehicles stay 20 ms. | HR multirate. Measured 2026-10-28 on the example quad: 10, 5, 2.5 and 1.25 ms give the same energy (±0.5 %) and hold error (±0.02 m), so 10 ms was already converged for it; 2.5 ms costs 3.2x the quad's wall time and buys headroom for stiffer frames. Receipt "Time step" re-checks 2.5 vs 1.25 ms. |
| 8 | Seawater density is TEOS-10 in situ from HYCOM T/S, applied to buoyancy only; drag, fin lift and wave pressure keep 1025 kg/m³. Gliders are re-ballasted neutral for their launch-point water (user's choice, 2026-10-28), keeping any offset the design had from neutral; other subs keep their mass. Hulls are rigid (no compressibility). T/S is one HYCOM snapshot nearest the start, held for the mission. | Buoyancy is where ±0.5 % of density matters: at TEPEX the 100 m water lifts a trimmed glider by 0.23 kg, 57 % of its 400 cc engine. In drag/lift it is a 0.5 % change, inside the CFD band. A real glider hull compresses and cancels part of the pressure term (~0.45 kg/m³ per 100 m), so rigid is slightly pessimistic for diving. One snapshot: tds.hycom.org took 330 s for four 3-hourly T/S times of a 1° box (over the 300 s deadline) and 39 s for one, and 3-hourly fields don't resolve the internal waves that move density faster anyway. |
| 9 | The surface drone stays a motor boat until a saildrone model exists. | The spec's surface drone is a saildrone; nothing in the repo has a sail. Logged as missing in STATUS. |
| 10 | Gliders keep the built-in controller (a stub in the spec's terms). | ArduSub has no buoyancy-engine or fin control; flagged in STATUS. |
| 11 | Planner self-check asserts the spec's air/ocean reference rows (one L2a-sized nest per medium, 20 % of budget, at 10⁹ cell-updates/s/GPU); hull rows are shown, not asserted. Real plans use `bench/throughput.json`. | Those four numbers reproduce to the spec's quoted digits. The spec's hull rows came from an L3 cost model it didn't state; the graded L3 here gives 1.5–2x different cells, and the spec text isn't on disk to reconcile. The 10⁹ default can be 5x off either way. |
