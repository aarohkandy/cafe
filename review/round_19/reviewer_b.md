### B-4. The step effect of the current columns on a drifting Slocum's battery margin has a mechanism, which the code comment says nobody found: the last surfacing before the window ends; the two columns count towards the noise floor
severity: minor
lane: physics / documentation (the open item of the addendum)
where: `cafe/pm/stage1.py:58` ("no mechanism was identified"), `cafe/pm/stage1.py:68` (`DEMO_ONLY`), `cafe/pm/physics/glider.py:459-470` (the `reach` and `up` energy terms) and `:494` (the battery flag), DECISIONS 992
reproduction: `probe_b1.py`, a toy Slocum (platform `slocum`, drift, heading 0, 500 m dives, dt 60 s, 6 d from -170, 2 N, one member) in an `AnalyticEnv` whose density is the CI-like stratification plus a horizontal gradient of 0.01 kg m-3 per degree of longitude, the current scaled 0 to 3 times 0.15 m s-1:
- gradient 0 and 1e-3: nothing changes at any scale (55 dives completed, 55 surfacings, E 720994.70 J at every scale): inert, as on the CI fixture.
- gradient 0.01: nothing changes until scale 1.5, where the member completes 54 surfacings instead of 55 (dives that reach depth: 55 in both) and E falls from 720994.70 to 719247.49 J, a step of 1747.2 J. One surfacing costs `comm_J` (3.5 W x (120 s + 250 s) + 414 J = 1709 J) plus the surface pump (150.83 cc x 101325 Pa / 0.4 = 38.2 J) = 1747.2 J: the step is that one event to 0.01 J. As a share of the usable pack (1.2e7 x 0.9 J) it is 1.62e-4, the 1.6e-4 DECISIONS 992 measured on the demo (its 1.6147e-4 to 1.6154e-4 = one surfacing at a battery scale near 1 and a dive-trim of about 138 cc).
So: the current moves the glider's position; in a gridded ocean the density it sees differs (horizontal density gradient), which changes its vertical speed by a part in 1e4 and shifts the cycle (about 9,400 s) so that over 55 cycles the last ascent finishes before the window end in one run and after it in the other. The event is the number of completed surfacings in the window, quantised by the 60 s step and by the window end; it is physics in that a faster cycle really does surface once more, and a sampling artifact in that the battery margin read at a fixed T is a sawtooth in the cycle phase with a tooth of one surfacing. It is not a grid-interpolation artifact (the toy has no grid), and it needs a horizontal density gradient (the CI fixture has none, which is why it is inert there). The same mechanism lets any column that moves the position (the wind through the 1 % windage while surfaced, about 30 times less) produce the same step with a smaller probability; the demo probe's 8 members x 5 quantiles cannot exclude it, so "the wind columns are float noise" (comment, stage1.py:59-60) is also a statement about the probe's size.
Whether the columns belong: a step of 1.6e-4 of the pack against a member spread of 5.2e-3 (3 %) carries no ranking information, while two more columns raise the permutation noise floor of that row: for n = 64 members of independent draws the 95 % floor is 0.155 for 2 columns and 0.186 for 4 (mean of 5 seeds, `sensitivity.noise_floor`, 500 permutations), so a Slocum's battery ranking is held to 0.03 more of S1 for a column pair that acts in a few members at the tail quantiles. Dropping them (they are 'DEMO_ONLY': no CI test holds them either) would not change a q20 number (glider_2n passes in every member, `all_pass`); keeping them is a choice, but the comment and DECISIONS 992 should say the mechanism, and that it is a count of surfacings at the window end, not a response of the battery to the current.
wrong data: no (the only output is a battery-failure sensitivity line of a drifting Slocum, which q20 does not print)
